TasFarmers · Sea Forest · Tasmanian Institute of Agriculture

Cutting methane from Tasmanian livestock while keeping farms profitable.

A Tasmanian Government–funded trial testing whether lick block supplementation can improve productivity in Tasmanian livestock, while the Sea Forest SeaFeed™ it contains helps reduce greenhouse gas emissions, with cost and performance data published throughout the trial rather than after its completion.

How it works

1

The opportunity

Livestock digestion produces methane, a greenhouse gas that is significantly more potent than CO₂. Reducing these emissions is one of the greatest opportunities for Tasmanian and global agriculture but, until now, has been difficult to achieve without compromising farm productivity.

2

The idea

KleenGrow lick blocks, or if preferred loose licks, containing Sea Forest's SeaFeed™ are provided to livestock as part of their normal diet. Research has demonstrated that the active compound can significantly reduce the methane produced by cattle during digestion.

3

The goal

This trial is testing whether that promise holds up on commercial farms by assessing the cost-effectiveness of reducing emissions against the cost and impact on herd performance, while demonstrating that this practice is both environmentally and economically sustainable.

Why this trial exists

Real promise, tested on working farms

Asparagopsis seaweed-based supplements have demonstrated considerable promise in reducing livestock methane emissions under controlled research settings. What remains untested at a commercial scale in Tasmania is whether these benefits can be achieved on working farms and at what cost. This is the purpose of the trial.

Backed by a $4 million grant from the Tasmanian Government, TasFarmers partnered with the Tasmanian Institute of Agriculture, and with Sea Forest as the manufacturer of the supplement, to run the trial on participating farms and capture the data.

The objective is simple to state but challenging to deliver: measure the emissions reduction, assess it against the real costs and quantify herd performance, and publish a transparent result that every Tasmanian livestock producer can use, whether or not they participated in the trial.
