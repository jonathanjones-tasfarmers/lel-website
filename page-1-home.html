How it works

1

The opportunity

Livestock digestion produces methane, a greenhouse gas many times more potent than CO₂ in the short term. It's one of the biggest levers Tasmanian agriculture has for cutting emissions, and one of the hardest to pull without hurting farm output.

2

The idea

Sea Forest's Asparagopsis seaweed lick blocks are fed to livestock as part of their normal diet. Research proves the compound can significantly cut the methane cattle produce during digestion.

3

The goal

This trial is testing whether that promise holds up on real commercial farms, weighing the emissions cut against the actual cost and impact on herd performance, not just in a lab.

Why this trial exists

Real promise, tested on a working farm

Asparagopsis seaweed supplements have shown real promise in reducing methane from livestock in controlled research settings. What hadn't been tested at commercial scale in Tasmania was whether that promise holds up on a working farm, and what it actually costs.

That's what this trial is for. Backed by a $4 million grant from the Tasmanian Government, TasFarmers partnered with the Tasmanian Institute of Agriculture for the science and Sea Forest as the supplement's manufacturer to run the trial on the ground and capture the data.

The brief is simple to state and hard to deliver: estimate the emissions reduction, weigh it against the real cost and productivity impact, and publish an honest answer, one every Tasmanian livestock producer can use, whether or not they were part of the trial.

Looking ahead

Where wool could fit in

Beef leads this trial today. Wool is where it's aiming to go next. Milestone 5 of the project's Grant Deed includes scope for a wool-focused sheep cohort, tracking fibre and product quality using a similar on-farm delivery model and monitoring infrastructure to what's already proven on beef. No wool producers are confirmed yet, this is a stated ambition, not a running program, and it's an open door for millers, brands and industry bodies who want to help shape what that looks like.

Our mission

Proving emissions and profitability aren't a trade-off

Prove, on working Tasmanian farms and in the open, whether cutting livestock methane can go hand in hand with a profitable herd. Backed by a $4 million Tasmanian Government grant, TasFarmers, the Tasmanian Institute of Agriculture and Sea Forest are testing Sea Forest's Asparagopsis seaweed supplement on commercial properties, not in a lab, publishing the real cost and performance data as the trial runs. The goal: an honest answer every Tasmanian producer can use, whether or not they took part.
